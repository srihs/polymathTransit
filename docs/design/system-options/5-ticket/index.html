1 About this direction

Ticket. Every trip is a ticket: a compact card where each fact always sits in the same place – date, pick up, going to, passengers, trip for – with the return below a perforation. The form fills a ticket, the confirmation issues it, the coordinator's day is a rack of tickets along each van's line, and the driver punches each stop's stub as the day goes.

It takes its principles from the card tickets Sri Lanka Railways still issues (every fact in a fixed place, named by a serial number) and from Tyler Thompson's "Boarding Pass / Fail" (order by what the traveller needs; give the time its own place). The numbers are printed in Stick No Bills, a stencil face from Mooniak in Colombo made for a Galle poster gallery.

The rule behind the look: the system prints in the stencil; people write and read in Plex Sans Condensed.

Principles

  1. Every fact has a fixed place. The same slot order on the form, the confirmation, the dashboard and the driver's stub.
  2. The reference is the ticket's name. Always visible, and the part people say aloud (0142) is the largest.
  3. Outbound and return are two parts of one ticket, divided by a perforation. Perforations divide nothing else.
  4. Done is a punch, not a page. A stop is finished with a round hole and a time, not a new screen.

2 Logo

The full logo carries its own white sticker, so it sits on the desk ground as it is. It appears at 240 px wide or more. The product name PolymathTransit is set in Plex Sans Condensed 600 beside the crest; the college's name only ever appears through the logo artwork.

Clear space and smallest sizes

Clear space: a quarter of the crest's height on every side (16 px around a 64 px crest), shown by the dashed box.

  • Crest: 24 px tall at the smallest (phone headers use 32 px).
  • Full logo: 240 px wide at the smallest, so the tagline stays readable.
  • Favicon: crest.png.

Two things never to do

  • Do not stamp the crest. It is never drawn inside a status stamp, recoloured to a status colour, rotated like a rubber stamp or punched; the crest is the college's mark, not a ticket mark.
  • Do not print the college's name in the stencil. "Polymath College" is never typeset in Stick No Bills or Plex as if it were the logo; only references, times and counts are printed in the stencil.

3 Colour

The crest purple leads and is kept for three jobs: the issued ticket's strip, primary actions and links. Tickets lie on a desk ground and are separated from it by their edge alone, with no shadows. 4 Type

IBM Plex Sans Condensed (400, 600) for Latin text: narrow and engineered, so long labels fit fixed slots. Yaldevi (400, 600) for Sinhala: about 30% narrower than Noto at the same legibility, which matters on a 360 px phone. Stick No Bills 600 for references, issued times and counts in both scripts – never for words, and never below 20 px, where the stencil's breaks start to eat the figures. Times use tabular figures.

5 Spacing and grid

A 4 px base. Space groups facts; it is never used to impress. Inside a ticket, slots sit edge to edge with hairlines between them, like the printed boxes on a card ticket.

The ticket is a size container: under 420 px it lays its slots in 2 columns, from 420 px in 3, from 760 px in 6. Corners: 4 px on tickets and controls, 2 px on stamps. No shadows; notches are 8 px radius.

6 Icons

Drawn for this direction like rubber-stamp marks: outline, 24 px grid with a 2 px live-area margin, 1.75 px stroke, round ends and joins, simple closed shapes, no fills except the punched hole. Early trip and Out of hours are a pair: a clock with its line before or after it. An icon always sits beside its word.

8 The signature element: the ticket

The ticket does one job: it makes a trip the same object for everyone who handles it. Staff check their request on it before sending; the confirmation issues it with its reference; the coordinator sees each booking as a small ticket on its van's line and opens it as a full ticket with the explanation on the stub; the driver punches a stub at each stop. Because the date, pick up, going to, passengers and trip-for slots are always in the same order, a coordinator reading a ticket aloud on the phone and a requester holding theirs are looking at the same place for the same fact. The purple strip means one thing only: this ticket is issued (confirmed).

9 Motion

Motion only explains a change. Nothing loops, nothing moves on scroll. With "reduce motion" set on the device, every change is instant.
