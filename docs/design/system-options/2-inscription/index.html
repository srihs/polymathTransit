Direction two

Inscription

The college speaks in its own voice. Capitals from the wordmark name things; the italic of “Learn to Live” guides and explains; lime-washed grounds and one deep verandah band give each page its calm.

About this direction

Inscription makes PolymathTransit feel like the college, not like software. Classical inscriptional capitals, taken from the “POLYMATH COLLEGE” wordmark, name things briefly. The humanist italic of the tagline becomes the voice that guides and explains. And the calm of Sri Lankan tropical modernism, composed light and shade, gives the pages their space: a lime-washed ground, and one deep shaded band that frames what matters most on each page.

The signature is the step ladder: the crest’s stepped top becomes four rising steps that show where a request stands and what happens next.

Principles

  1. Speak as the college: courteous, brief, never chatty.
  2. Capitals name; italic guides; roman does the work. Capitals are for names of four words or fewer; never for sentences.
  3. One deep band frames what matters most on each page; everything else is calm.
  4. Every colour comes from a material and has a job: leaf, laterite, brick and slate each mean one status.

Two things never to do

  • Never put crest.png (the crest without its keyline) on the band or on purple: the shield disappears. Use crest-keyline.png there.
  • Never set “Polymath College” in Marcellus or any typeface as if it were the logo, and never stretch, recolour, outline or animate the logo. The product name “PolymathTransit” is set in Marcellus capitals; the college’s name appears only through the logo artwork.

Type

Marcellus capitals (Astigmatic, “inspired by classic Roman inscription letterforms”) name things. Ysabeau Office (Christian Thalmann), a humanist sans from the Garamond tradition with a true calligraphic italic, carries the text and the voice. Sinhala headings use Abhaya Libre (Mooniak with Pushpananda Ekanayake, from the 1960s letterpress faces); Sinhala text uses Noto Serif Sinhala.

Signature: the step ladder

The crest’s stepped top becomes the booking’s progress mark: four rising steps, Submitted, Proposed, Confirmed and Completed, each filled as the booking reaches it and named in words. Its job is to tell a non-technical person where their request stands and what happens next, using a shape that already belongs to the college. Outcomes off the ladder (Needs attention, Declined, Cancelled, No-show) show as a step that drops below the line, with the word. The same shape, at 16 px, is the glyph in every status badge and on every timeline block, so status is always carried by shape as well as colour. It is drawn from plain rectangles; the crest is never traced.
