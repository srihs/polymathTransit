Departures

Time is the backbone. Every list reads like a departure board: one line per trip, fixed columns, and the status as a short word in the remarks slot. The coordinator's dashboard is a dark board for the 7:05 am approval; the phone pages stay light for sunlight, with one dark board strip for the most important line.

1About this direction

Departures borrows the one idea that fifty years of station and airport boards have proved: people find their trip fastest when every trip is a single line, the time comes first, the columns never move, and the status is a short word in its own slot. PolymathTransit uses that line everywhere a trip appears, so a teacher choosing a date, a coordinator approving the day and a driver checking the next stop all read the same shape.

The only dark surface is the board. It earns its place on the coordinator's desktop at 7:05 am, when the day must be checked at a glance. Phones stay light for sunlight and carry a single board strip for the line that matters most: the reference, or the next stop.

Principles

  1. Time leads every line.
  2. One line per trip; the same columns everywhere.
  3. Status is a remark: one or two words in its own slot, with a shape.
  4. Glance first, read second.

Clear space and smallest sizes

  • Clear space on every side: one quarter of the crest's height (the dashed frame above: 24 px around a 96 px crest).
  • Crest: 24 px tall at the smallest. In this direction it is 36 px in phone headers and 32 px on the dashboard.
  • Full logo: 240 px wide at the smallest.

Two things not to do

  • Do not put the plain crest on the dark board or on purple. Its purple shield disappears (purple on the board is 2.16:1). Use the keyline crest there.
  • Do not set "Polymath College" in B612 or any font as a logo. The college's name appears only through the logo artwork; the product name "PolymathTransit" is set in B612 Bold beside the crest.

Never recolour, stretch, rotate, outline, shadow or animate the logo or crest.

3Colour

Two themes share one set of semantic names. Light is for phones and sunlight; the board is for the coordinator's desktop and the single strip at the top of each phone page. Every colour other than the brand purple, the logo grey, black, white and the neutrals has a job: a status. Ratios use the WCAG formula; AA needs 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large text and parts of controls; AAA needs 7:1 for text.

4Type

B612 and B612 Mono were drawn for aircraft cockpit screens and tested in degraded conditions; capitals, figures and abbreviations stay distinct, which matters for references such as PT-2026-0142 and phone numbers. All times, counts and references are set in B612 Mono, so they line up in columns like a board. Sinhala is set in Gemunu Libre (Mooniak), whose smooth, squared edge sits with B612's instrument shapes.

Board rows are 40 px on the desktop and 56 px or more on phones. Corners are 2 px. There are no shadows: the board is flat, and cells are divided by 1 px board lines.

8Signature: the board row and the now line

One trip, one line
TimeRefFrom → ToVanPaxRemarksFlags
8:40 amPT-2026-0142Branch B → Sports CentreVan 24Proposed
  • Shared

One row format is used wherever a trip appears: the time first in mono, then the places, the van, the passengers and the remark slot. On the dashboard the same rows form the list view, the tiles are the board's header strip, and the day chart carries a vertical "now" line at 7:05 am that every run reads against. On the request form the date choice is a small departures list: each date is a row with its remark ("Closed", "Closes 5:00 pm today", "Open"), so the cutoff is part of the choice itself (FR-07). On the driver's phone the dark strip at the top is the next stop's row, with the time large and "in 20 min". Its job: make time and status readable at a glance, in the same place, on every screen.
